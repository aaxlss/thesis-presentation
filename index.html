The Challenge in B2B Sales

  • Sales departments waste effort on low-conversion prospects.
  • Goal: Transform static pipelines into dynamic, probability-based early warning systems.
  • Requires interpretable models for real-world sales strategy.
B2B sales cycles are long and expensive. Sales teams waste huge amounts of time on leads that will never convert. The goal of this research is to transform static CRMs into dynamic early-warning systems using machine learning, but this requires models that are not just accurate, but also highly interpretable for sales teams.

Research Questions

  1. Architecture: Do modern deep learning models outperform traditional ensemble methods on tabular CRM data?
  2. Data Efficiency: How does reducing feature sets (from 100% down to 25%) impact predictive accuracy?
  3. Interpretability (HCAIM): How can XAI tools convert mathematical outputs into human-readable recommendations?
  4. Ethics (HCAIM): How do we balance high-accuracy modeling with B2B privacy and GDPR's "Right to an Explanation"?
Highlight the four pillars of the research: 1. Architecture: Tree-based models vs Deep Learning. 2. Data Efficiency: How much data do we actually need? 3. Interpretability: Can we explain the AI's decisions? 4. Ethics: Balancing accuracy with GDPR compliance.

Phase 1: Feature Selection

Correlation-Based Redundancy Removal to mitigate multicollinearity.

  • Computed Pearson correlation matrix.
  • Generated experimental subsets based on correlation limits:
  • 75% Subset: Moderate filter, preserves majority of signal.
  • 25% Subset: Aggressive filtration, nearly independent variables.
Moving to the technical methodology. To reduce noise, we used Pearson correlation to remove redundant variables. We created two experimental subsets: a 75% subset (moderate filter) and a 25% subset (aggressive filter).

Phase 2 & 3: Partitioning

  • 80/20 Stratified Train-Test Split.
  • Training Data (80%): Used for model learning and balancing.
  • Testing Data (20%): Completely untouched, preserves native "Won"/"Loss" distribution for honest evaluation.
Standard 80/20 train-test split, stratified to ensure the 77/22 class imbalance is maintained in both sets.

Phase 2 & 3: Data Balancing

Applied strictly to Training Data.

  • SMOTE: Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique via nearest-neighbor interpolation.
  • ADASYN: Adaptive Synthetic sampling focusing on hard-to-learn minority instances.
To fix the class imbalance, I tested SMOTE and ADASYN. SMOTE synthesizes points between existing minority examples, while ADASYN focuses specifically on the "hard-to-learn" examples near the decision boundary.

Phase 4: AI Modeling & Tuning

  • Pipeline developed in Python 3 (scikit-learn, XGBoost, PyTorch).
  • Hyperparameter tuning optimized for F1 Score to balance precision and recall.
  • Overcoming the risk of models simply guessing "Loss" every time.
Explain that the pipeline was built in Python. Models were optimized for the F1 score, not just Accuracy, because I need a balance between Precision and Recall to avoid the "always predict Loss" trap.

Phase 4: Models Compared

Traditional Machine Learning

  • Random Forest: Bagging ensemble, stable baseline.
  • XGBoost: Gradient-boosting framework. The benchmark standard.

Modern Deep Learning

  • FT-Transformer: Feature tokenization with multi-head self-attention.

Introduction of the models: Random Forest and XGBoost. Deep Learning designed for tabular data: FT-Transformer (attention mechanism) and TabNet (sequential sparse attention).

Phase 5: Evaluation Criteria

  • Accuracy: Percentage of overall correct predictions (can be misleading).
  • F1 Score: Balance of Precision (correct wins) and Recall (catching all possible wins).
  • ROC-AUC: Threshold-independent discriminative ability (1.0 is perfect).
Reiterate why we use multiple metrics. Accuracy can hide flaws. F1 Score proves the model can actually identify "Wins". ROC-AUC shows overall discriminative power.

The "Deep Learning Gap"

Result: Traditional tree-based models decisively outclassed Modern Deep Learning.

  • XGBoost proved highly precise in distinguishing success vs. failure.
  • Deep Learning models plateaued (~86.8% accuracy) and struggled severely with full feature sets.
  • Data Efficiency: Top models maintained accuracy at 75% features but collapsed at 25%.
Address the first Research Question. Traditional tree-based models decisively beat Modern Deep Learning. XGBoost thrived, while FT-Transformer and TabNet struggled to exceed 86.8% accuracy. Also note Data Efficiency: XGBoost survived the 25% feature reduction much better than the neural networks.

Accuracy vs. GDPR Compliance

The Ethical Accuracy-Calibration Paradox

  • XGBoost: Most accurate overall.
  • Random Forest: Yielded a vastly lower Expected Calibration Error (ECE - 0.0326 vs 0.0674).
  • Takeaway: Random Forest provides more "trustworthy" probability scores, aligning better with GDPR's "Right to an Explanation".
Address the Ethics question. While XGBoost is the most accurate, Random Forest hads a much better Expected Calibration Error (ECE). This means Random Forest's probability scores are more trustworthy. If a client demands a GDPR explanation for an automated rejection, Random Forest is arguably the safer, more ethical choice despite a tiny drop in raw accuracy.
Expected Calibration Error (ECE) is a metric used to evaluate how well a model's predicted probabilities reflect the actual probabilities of outcomes
ECE is a metric that quantifies the gap between a model's predicted confidence (the probability it assigns to its prediction) and its actual accuracy.
A perfectly calibrated model has an ECE of 0, meaning its confidence always perfectly matches its accuracy.

Conclusion & Future Work

  • Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) remains the apex for tabular B2B forecasting.
  • Transparency and Model Actionability dictate practical success.
  • Future Work: Incorporating unstructured data (emails/transcripts) via NLP and expanding hardware scaling.
Wrap up. Tree models rule tabular CRM data. We proved high accuracy is possible, but we must choose models that offer XAI capabilities to satisfy GDPR and build trust with human sales teams. Future work could explore Temporal Neural Networks for time-series pipeline data. Thank the audience.
