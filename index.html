A Comparative Analysis of Machine Learning and Deep Learning Architectures in B2B Sales Prediction

Alejandro Axel Sanchez Silva

Human-Centred AI Masters

The Challenge in B2B Sales

  • Sales departments waste effort on low-conversion prospects.
  • CRM Data shows severe class imbalance (77% Losses vs 22% Wins).
  • Goal: Transform static pipelines into dynamic, probability-based early warning systems.

Data Preparation

  • 78,025 total records from an automotive CRM.
  • Balancing applied only to training data to prevent leakage.
  • SMOTE: Synthetic oversampling via interpolation.
  • ADASYN: Focuses on hard-to-learn minority instances.

Research Questions

  1. Traditional vs. Deep Learning performance?
  2. Impact of feature reduction & key predictive drivers?
  3. Translating AI outputs to practical sales insights?
  4. Balancing accuracy with ethical GDPR compliance?

RQ1: Traditional vs. Modern Architectures

Result: Traditional tree-based models decisively outclassed Modern Deep Learning in tabular CRM context.

  • XGBoost (100% features + SMOTE): 89.57% Accuracy, 0.939 ROC-AUC.
  • FT-Transformer: Collapsed completely utilizing full features (Worst performer).
  • Highlights the "Deep Learning Gap" - complex models failed without immense structure tuning.

RQ2: Feature Selection & Key Drivers

Result: Surprising resilience to data reduction limits.

  • Top models maintained peak accuracy using just 75% of features.
  • Performance steeply collapses when restricted to 25%.
  • Top Drivers: Deal Velocity (Days Qualified), Opportunity Financial Value, and Client Relationship History.

RQ3: XAI & Practical Insights

Result: Making opaque models transparent.

  • Permutation Importance: Flagged deal duration as a macro-level barrier.
  • SHAP: Quantified exactly how deal size impacts the baseline probability of closing.
  • LIME: Provides "human-readable" local logic dictating why an individual deal was scored highly for sales reps.

RQ4: Accuracy vs. GDPR Compliance

Result: The Ethical Accuracy-Calibration Paradox.

  • XGBoost is the most accurate (guesses most wins correctly).
  • Random Forest yielded vastly lower Expected Calibration Error (ECE - 0.0326 vs 0.0674).
  • Takeaway: Random Forest gives more trustworthy probability scores per deal, making it better suited for GDPR's "Right to an Explanation."

Actionable Intelligence

  • Pivot to Resellers: (27.6% Win Rate) away from Field Sales (18.6%).
  • New Client Hurdle: Utilize heavy introductory discounts since repeat clients have >82% win rates.
  • Opportunity Health: Track stagnant stages as rapid phase movements correspond with higher closes.

Conclusion

  • Gradient Boosting approaches remain the "end game" for tabular B2B forecasting.
  • Transparency trumps raw accuracy: Model actionability guides strategy.
  • Using models like Random Forest builds trust & keeps humans centered in the loop.
